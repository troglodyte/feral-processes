# Your character

A run opens on seven questions. Answer them in order, Esc walks back one, and Esc on the first
hands you back to the main menu. Nothing is decided until you confirm the summary at the end.

R rolls every choice you have not made yet and jumps straight to that summary — except the first
one. Difficulty is a commitment rather than a shape, so it is never rolled for you, and R before
you have picked it is refused.

Difficulty is the only answer with no way back. On Permadeath, flatlining ends the run and archives
the session to a log. On Forgiving you reboot and keep going, at a cost. Everything else on the
wizard is a preference; this is the rule the whole run is played under.

Your class is a spread of affinity multipliers and nothing else — no stats, no talent tree, no
levels. An affinity scales the authored power of your own routines by category: damage, repair,
buffs, afflictions and drains. There are five, and every one of them raises one category and holds
a different one down, so a class is a trade rather than a bonus.

- Striker hits hardest and repairs worst.
- Bastion buffs hardest and hits softest.
- Medic repairs hardest and hits softest.
- Saboteur afflicts hardest and repairs worst.
- Leech drains hardest and buffs softest.

What a class does not touch is your ordinary swing. Attacking is not a routine, so a damped
category costs you nothing until you invoke something in that category — which is also why picking
a class you will not build around is a quiet way to pay for nothing. Each class brings its own
opening kit as well, so the pack you land with is a second thing the choice decides.

Your look is a glyph and a colour, and it is only a look. Pick both from the one list — the cursor
walks icons and swatches together, Enter takes whichever you are standing on, and n moves along
once you are happy. The preview in the corner is drawn the same way the map draws you.

Points are five, spent on top of what every process starts with rather than shuffling it around.
Left and right adjust the highlighted axis, Shift with them empties or fills it, and Enter takes
the spend as it stands — you may leave points unspent, and the screen opens on a roll rather than a
blank form so there is something to argue with.

- Integrity — one point buys six Integrity, and your current Integrity rises with it.
- Attack — one point buys one Attack.
- Decompiler — one point buys one Decompiler, which is your odds of compiling a beaten program.
- Mitigation — three points buy one point of Mitigation. It costs more because levelling never
  raises it, so unlike the other three it is a number nothing else in the run will grow for you.

Your starter routine fills the free slot. Two slots is what everybody opens with and Decompile
already holds one of yours, so this is the other. The pool is not filtered by class — a Medic may
take a damage routine — and each row is priced through the class you already picked, which is the
cheapest place in the game to see what an affinity is actually worth. You keep the knowledge as
well as the install, so the routine can be etched onto a disk later like anything else you know. n
moves on and leaves the slot empty if you would rather choose later.

Your name is yours, and it is what the save list calls the run.

The summary is the last step and it confirms nothing until you press Enter. It also shows what your
achievement record is about to pay into this run — the stat points, the Perk Points, the program —
which is the one moment before a run starts where cross-run progress is worth reading.

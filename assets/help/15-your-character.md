# Your character

A run opens on eleven steps: difficulty, achievements, class, kit, icon, colour, points, perks, a
starter routine, a summary, and a name. Esc walks back one step, and Esc on the first hands you back
to the main menu. On the steps that do not spend anything, Left and Right also page back and
forward. Enter on the name starts the run.

Difficulty is the only answer with no way back. On Permadeath, flatlining ends the run and archives
the session to a log. On Forgiving you reboot and keep going, at a cost. Everything else on the
wizard is a preference; this is the rule the whole run is played under.

The achievements step has nothing to decide. It lists what your earlier runs are about to pay into
this one — stat points, Perk Points, a starting program — or says there is nothing yet. Achievements
carry into every run after the one that earns them.

Your class is a spread of affinity multipliers plus a starting set of attributes. An affinity
scales the authored power of your own routines by category: damage, repair, buffs, afflictions and
drains. There are eight classes, and every one holds damage, repair or buffs down to pay for what it
raises.

- Striker hits hardest and repairs worst.
- Bastion buffs hardest and hits softest.
- Medic repairs hardest and hits softest.
- Saboteur afflicts hardest and repairs worst.
- Leech drains hardest and buffs softest.
- Decompiler hits softer and lands every decompile attempt 15% more often.
- Fabricator buffs somewhat harder, hits softer, and finishes every work cycle in the base 20%
  sooner.
- Invoker buffs and afflicts a little harder, hits a little softer, and carries two more routine
  slots at every level.

What a class does not touch is your ordinary attack. Attacking is not a routine, so a damped
category costs you nothing until you invoke something in that category — which is also why picking
a class you will not build around is a quiet way to pay for nothing.

Your kit is twenty-five Credits and a shelf to spend them on. Left and right take and put back on
the highlighted row, Shift with them empties or fills it, Ctrl closes half the gap, and r throws the
basket away and draws a fresh one. The shelf carries materials, the three companion upgrades, a rest
charge and the plainest gear — nothing a run is meant to go out and earn. The step will not let you
leave while anything on the shelf is still affordable, so you spend the Credits rather than carry
them. If nothing left is cheap enough, the remainder arrives as Credits to spend on the first
trader you reach. The class has no kit of its own once you have been through this screen: what you
picked is your whole pack.

Your look is a glyph and a colour, and it is only a look. They come as two screens: five shapes,
then the swatches. Enter takes the row you are standing on and moves you along, and n walks past
without changing anything. A sixth row on the shape screen opens an editor to draw your own map
icon; the swatch still colours your glyph everywhere else. The preview in the corner follows the
cursor, so you can see an option before you take it, and it is drawn the same way the map draws
you.

Points are twenty, spent on top of the attributes your class starts with. The screen opens on a
random spread so there is something to argue with. Left and right adjust the highlighted attribute,
Shift with them empties or fills it, and each point costs one. Like the kit, the step will not let
you leave while a point is still affordable.

- Analysis — each point adds 1 attack and 1 decompiler skill, and a little to how often you mine a
  hard vein.
- Bandwidth — each point adds 2 to your maximum Power.
- Entropy — each point adds 0.2% to your crit chance and 0.1% to your fumble chance.
- Footprint — each point adds 1 mitigation and 2 maximum Integrity.
- Parity — each point adds 6 maximum Integrity.
- Persistence — each point shortens harmful statuses by 1%.

Levelling never raises mitigation, so Footprint is the one place a point of it can be bought
directly. See [attributes](attributes) for what the numbers mean.

Perks come next: four Perk Points to spend on the first perks, with the same Left, Right, Shift and
Ctrl keys. This step lets you leave with points in hand, and they arrive with the run.

Your starter routine fills a free slot. You open with two slots, four as an Invoker, and Decompile
already holds one of them, so this fills another. The pool is not filtered by class — a Medic may
take a damage routine — and each row is priced through the class you already picked, which is the
cheapest place in the game to see what an affinity is actually worth. You keep the knowledge as
well as the install, so the routine can be etched onto a disk later like anything else you know. n
moves on and leaves the slot empty if you would rather choose later.

The summary reads the whole character back, including the achievement payout, and Enter accepts
it. The last step is your name, which is what the save list calls the run. A blank name is fine.
Enter on it starts the run.

# Zones and breaching

A zone is one sector of the Grid: one map, one set of biomes, one population of rogue programs.
Every wild program carries the zone it was born in appended to its name, and each zone up is a
step harder than the last — a steady climb, not a doubling, so the levels it takes to keep pace
stay roughly the same however deep you go.

You leave a zone through a Zone Portal. Deploy one and walk onto it.

- A portal costs Portal Fragments — ten in zone 1, and half that again for every zone below the one
  you are standing in.
- Fragments come out of the Stack and nowhere else. Beating a lair's guardian underground is what
  pays for the way out, which is why a run that never goes down never breaches.
- The portal is consumed as you step through it. It is the one structure that does not make the
  trip, so every breach is a fresh build.
- There is no way back.

Fragments do not survive the crossing. Portal Fragments and Core Fragments are cleared as you step
through, so every zone has to fund its own exit; there is no farming zone 1 rich and chain-breaching
on the stockpile. Credits cross, and so does Research Data.

Everything else travels with you. Your party, every deployed structure, the platform floor under
them, your gear, your supplies and your fusion tiers all rematerialise around the new entry point in
the layout they left in — damage, node stock and running cronjobs included. Wild programs and nests
are left behind.

How much meets you at once is the zone's business, not the map's. Zone 1 is one program at a time,
which is what a run needs while your party is still empty; every zone after that widens the ceiling
on how large a group can get. Walking a long way from home does not make anything hit harder — the
only thing distance decides is the gentle ring around a fresh zone-1 landing, where nothing spawns
that a bare level-1 player could not beat one-on-one.

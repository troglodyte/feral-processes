# Your companions

A program joins you several ways: the one you start the run with, a decompile won in a fight, an
orphaned process adopted in the Stack for a catalyst, an orphan that sometimes survives a destroyed
nest and joins free, [breeding](breeding) two programs you already own, and fusing two of them
(below). However it arrived, it is yours permanently — there is nothing to feed and nothing to
renew.

Decompile reaches only the program next to you until you research more. Remote Decompile aims from
4 squares away, and Long-Range Decompile from 6; both need a Research Station and Remote Decompile
follows Routine Fabrication. Broadcast Decompile then spreads one decompile to the programs within 1
square of the target, and Wide Broadcast Decompile to those within 2. Each program it reaches rolls
separately and costs its own catalyst. See [your base](your-base) for how research is worked.

Roster slots start at three. A Data Cache adds five more for as long as it stands, and the Process
Pool perk adds one per level and cannot be taken back. Slots are not a limit: you can keep
capturing past them, but every program beyond your slots is unhappy about it, and an unhappy
program's morale slides until it throws tantrums.

Five of them can fight beside you at once. Everything else on the roster is base staff by
definition — there is no marker to set and no screen to set it on. Any program you own that is not
out fighting with you is working, which means your base's output grows with your roster rather than
with how carefully you assign it.

Programs level the way you do, up to the same ceiling: 6 in zone 1, 12 in zone 2, 23 in zone 3 and
11 more each zone after. Every level-up of a program puts its points into Parity and Analysis for
you, which raises its Integrity and Attack. A party you recruited early will still fall behind
unless you develop it, and developing it is four separate tracks.

- Kernel Rings. A ring opens two more talent tiers on one program, and three is the most any
  program can open. They are bought with Privilege Rings, which drop from a lair guardian
  underground and from nothing else, and the price climbs: one ring, then two, then three. A ring
  grants no stats and does not move the level ceiling.
- Talents. Every level a program earns above 6 pays one talent point into its class tree, two
  choices a tier, but only as many as its rings open: two per ring. Which tree it is comes from the
  program's class, so what a ring really buys is a program that is individual rather than merely
  bigger.
- Refactors. Five permanent percentage upgrades across Integrity, Attack and Mitigation, and five is
  the lifetime total across all three — a specialisation, not a shopping list. A Recompile Kernel is
  the separate one: it raises a program a zone tier and refuses once the program has caught up with
  the ground you are standing on.
- Gear. Any program you own wears a weapon, an armour and a module out of your own cargo, and a
  piece moves freely between them.

Fusing two programs makes one better one, once Program Refactoring is researched — see
[your base](your-base). Both have to be yours, they have to be two different programs, and neither
may already have been fused three times. There is no material cost, because losing two programs to
gain one is the cost.

- The higher-level parent is dominant: the child takes its species, its level, its Kernel Ring and
  its talents. The other parent's ring and talents are gone.
- Each stat is the better parent's, plus half the lesser's — always stronger than either alone,
  never the sum of both.
- Rarity, zone tier and refactor count all take the better of the two, so nothing is laundered back
  into a fresh program by fusing it.
- Every routine either parent was carrying that the winning species does not know innately is lost.
  The screen names them before you commit.
- You may name the result, and the name sticks everywhere.

Extracting a routine is the other thing that costs a program. It destroys the program and salvages
exactly one of its routines; everything else installed on it goes with it.

# Breeding

Breeding grows a new program from two of yours. It is a third way to add to your roster alongside
taming and fusing, and it is the only one that can produce a program with better rolls than either
parent had.

You need two things. A Breeding Bay is a structure that costs 14 Core Fragments and 2 Bytecode
Blocks, but you cannot build one until you have researched Bloodlines. Bloodlines is not on the
research list at first: once you have reached zone 2, studying a program can turn it up. A bay you
already built keeps working. A Breeding Seed is the item each breeding spends, and wild programs
shed them now and then, most often Glitches and Drones, then Scrappers and Worms. The seed has to
be in your pack. Seeds sitting in base storage do not count.

Once a bay stands, the base report (b) has a Breed page at the end of its rail, and standing
beside the bay, c opens the bay's own Breed page. (Its sheet, from x, takes you there too with B.)
Either page lists your programs and the bay's slot. You pick the first parent, then the second,
then confirm with Enter, and the seed is spent. With more than one bay, the base report breeds
into the first one with a free slot. Both parents have to be at the base. A program that is in your
party, out on a sortie or at an outpost is listed but greyed out, and so is one that is held (under
study, jailed, in a siphon), recovering in a repair bay, or already on its way to a bay. A program
cannot be paired with itself, a boss cannot breed, and a program that bred recently is listed but
refuses until it has rested. A bay holds one child at a time, and a slot stays taken from the moment
you confirm, so a second breeding waits until the first child has hatched.

Confirming does not start the child straight away. Both parents walk to the bay and stand beside it
together for about 30 seconds. While they are on this visit they do no work and their needs do not
drain. When the pause ends they go back to work, and only then does the child start growing. Each
parent keeps a memory of the other.

If something stops the visit, it is called off: a parent joins your party, goes on a sortie, is
knocked out or lost, cannot find a way to the bay, or the bay itself is demolished or destroyed. The
Breeding Seed goes back to your pack, the slot is freed, no memory forms and neither parent has to
rest. The log says who and why. A battle only pauses the visit.

The confirm page shows what you are about to get before anything is spent: the child's kind, its
generation, and the lowest and highest each of its rolls can land.

What the child is:

- Its kind. Two programs of the same kind breed that kind. A few pairs have a fixed hybrid, which
  is the only way to get it: a Worm and a Virus always breed a Botnet, and a Glitch and a Trojan
  always breed a Polymorph. Any other pair breeds one parent's kind, chosen at random.
- Level 1, Ordinary rarity, arriving at your anchor when it hatches. Hatching takes about ten minutes
  of play, counted from the end of the visit. If you already hold 200 programs it waits in the bay until there is room.
- A generation, shown on the program sheet. A child is one generation past its older parent, so
  breeding children of children builds a bloodline.
- Rolls taken from its parents. Each roll starts from the better of the two parents' and shifts a
  little either way. Every generation raises the ceiling a roll can reach, so a bloodline can
  read above 100% on the potential line, which no wild program ever does. The ceiling stops rising
  at 140%, so a long bloodline levels off there. Attributes are inherited the same way, and the
  ceiling on them rises for the first three generations only.

The parents are not used up. Both rest once the visit ends, for about as long as a hatch takes, and
during that time they can still fight, work and be fused. They only refuse to breed again.

A child still in its bay is not safe from a raid. A bay holding a growing child cannot be demolished, and a
siege or a sweep can still destroy it, and the log names each child lost with it. (A bay that is
only waiting on its parents can be demolished: the visit is called off and the seed comes back.) See [sieges](sieges).

# Breeding

Breeding grows a new program from two of yours. It is a third way to add to your roster alongside
taming and fusing, and it is the only one that can produce a program with better rolls than either
parent had.

You need two things. A Breeding Bay is a structure you can build from the start, with no research
in the way: 14 Core Fragments and 2 Bytecode Blocks. A Breeding Seed is the item each breeding
spends, and wild programs shed them now and then, most often Glitches and Drones, then Scrappers
and Worms.

Open the bay's sheet and press B. You pick the first parent, then the second, then confirm with
Enter, and the seed is spent. The parents come from your whole roster, wherever they are standing.
A program cannot be paired with itself, a boss cannot breed, and a program that bred recently is
listed but refuses until it has rested. A bay holds one child at a time, so a second breeding waits
for the first to hatch.

The confirm page shows what you are about to get before anything is spent: the child's kind, its
generation, and the lowest and highest each of its rolls can land.

What the child is:

- Its kind. Two programs of the same kind breed that kind. A few pairs have a fixed hybrid, which
  is the only way to get it: a Worm and a Virus always breed a Botnet, and a Glitch and a Trojan
  always breed a Polymorph. Any other pair breeds one parent's kind, chosen at random.
- Level 1, Ordinary rarity, arriving at your anchor when it hatches. Hatching takes about ten minutes
  of play. If you already hold 200 programs it waits in the bay until there is room.
- A generation, shown on the program sheet. A child is one generation past its older parent, so
  breeding children of children builds a bloodline.
- Rolls taken from its parents. Each roll starts from the better of the two parents' and shifts a
  little either way. Every generation raises the ceiling a roll can reach, so a deep bloodline can
  read above 100% on the potential line, which no wild program ever does. Attributes are inherited
  the same way, and the ceiling on them rises for the first few generations only.

The parents are not used up. Both rest for about as long as a hatch takes, and during that time
they can still fight, work and be fused. They only refuse to breed again.

A child still in its bay is not safe from a raid. A bay holding one cannot be demolished, but a
siege or a sweep can destroy it, and the log names each child lost with it. See [sieges](sieges).

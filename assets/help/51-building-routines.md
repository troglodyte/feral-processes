# Building routines

Routines you run are built, not found. At a Workbench you compile Subroutines, then combine them into
a routine and give it a name. Stand beside the Workbench and press c; its pages are Build routine,
Subroutines and Built routines.

A Subroutine is one part. Every routine has exactly one Core, which is what it does: Heal, Damage,
Drain and so on. Modifiers change how it is delivered: a Range, an Area, a Trickle that stretches it
over more rounds, a damage type, or a status. A Core takes only the modifiers that make sense for
it, and the Build routine page says why when a row is closed.

- Subroutines page. Every part, with how many you hold and what one costs: a blank Routine Disk and
  some of one material. A Core costs more material at a higher version. Type to filter the list and
  press Enter to compile one. A part you have not researched says so on its row.
- Build routine page. Press Enter on a row to see the researched parts that fit it and how many you
  hold; a part you have not researched is not listed, even if you hold one. The
  preview underneath reads out what the routine will do, its Power cost and how long before it can run
  again, which parts it uses and which you are missing. Name it, then choose Compile. Building uses up the
  parts.
- Built routines page. Routines you have built that are not in a slot yet.

A routine may hold only so many parts; research raises the limit.

To put a built routine to work, open the routines screen under m, pick yourself or a companion, pick an
empty slot and choose it. Popping a routine back out of a slot destroys it, and nothing comes back, so
think before you clear one.

Breaking a program down at a Workbench gives you the Subroutines of one of its routines, as items you
can use straight away.

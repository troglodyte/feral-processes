# Staff morale

Every program on your base staff has needs, and a mood. Programs out fighting beside you have a
mood too, but their needs do not run down. Open a program's manifest from the party menu to read them: the WORK
box lists its needs, and the MEMORIES box leads with its Mood.

Needs

There are two, and each is a reserve that starts full and runs down, faster while the program is
working. The manifest names the reserve as steady, fraying, strained or critical.

- Coherence is restored at a Log Analyzer Bay, which costs 12 Core Fragments. The program lies down
  and is defragmented.
- Slack is restored at a Sandbox, which costs 10 Core Fragments. The program goes and idles.

When a reserve runs low the program leaves its post, walks to the nearest machine that restores it,
and goes back to work once it is well restored. Until then it works a little worse. Neither building
takes a program to build, and both can stand inside a room; see [rooms](rooms) for how a room's
quality speeds the refill. A reserve that drops low (below 20 Coherence or 25 Slack) with nothing in the base to restore it, or with
the machine walled off, is logged. Once the base has 8 staff and 8 structures, that also leaves the
program with a grudge, against the walled-off corner if there was a machine it could not reach.
Programs differ in how fast they run down and how hard they hold a grudge. Nothing names the
difference, but you will see some programs at the Sandbox more than others.

Mood

Mood is the sum of what a program remembers, and the manifest reads it as devoted, content, even,
uneasy or bitter, with a number beside it. Memories fade unless the same thing happens again, and
repeats stack up to a limit. Bad ones come from being posted where nothing can reach, being swept
by a GC Entropy Sweep, working a machine that stays backed up, cutting rock for a long stretch, a
friend being lost or let go, being held in a Power Siphon, and having more programs than the roster
has slots for. Good ones come from winning a hard fight beside another program, a stretch at a
machine that is running well, standing on a comfortable floor finish, and time spent with other
programs. The MEMORIES box lists the strongest few.

What a bad mood does, in order as it worsens:

- Sulking. The program still works, but it refuses a post at a machine it holds a grudge against,
  and at any machine with a rival of its own standing beside it. Once the base has 8 staff and 8
  structures, an idle sulker now and then spoils one unit of output at a machine it resents or one
  a rival is working, and the log says which. If the base has a Log Analyzer Bay or a
  Sandbox it can reach, a sulking program walks there and stands about instead of working, and
  comes away fonder of the place, which lifts its mood. With none, it stays on the line.
- Downing tools. The program takes no job at all until it recovers, and the base panel notes the
  shortfall. A program does not have to be happy to return, only clear of the bad stretch.
- Lashing out. A program this low, with another within three tiles, may round on it. The fight
  lasts a few turns, and each blow takes a quarter of the target's Integrity, but never leaves it
  below 1. Whoever comes off worst is sent to a Repair Bay. The aggressor comes out calmer, and
  the one it hit does not forget. Programs that saw it hold it against the aggressor too.

A few bad memories are enough to make a program sulk, but downing tools takes a pattern of several, and
lashing out takes worse. The exceptions are being left past your roster slots and being held in a
Power Siphon, either of which can do it alone if left long enough. Both are fixed by you: build a
Data Cache, or release the program from the siphon.

What you can do about it

- Build a Log Analyzer Bay and a Sandbox early, near where the crew works, and keep them reachable.
  A base with neither offers a bad mood nowhere to go.
- Lay a comfortable floor finish in rooms where programs idle.
- Keep a program away from a machine it resents, or from a rival, by posting it elsewhere. The base
  staff table lets you uncheck jobs for a program.
- Keep the roster within its slots, and never leave a program in a Power Siphon longer than the
  power is worth.
- Wait. Memories fade, and a sulking program recovers once enough of them have.

Friends and rivals

Every program holds an opinion of each other program it knows, built from the same memories. Press
Tab on a program's manifest to switch between STATS, SOCIAL and TALK. The SOCIAL tab lists its
relationships, each with a standing and a number. A standing is Enemy at -15 or below, Rival from
-3, Neutral, Friend from +8 and Close from +20. It comes from fighting side by side, spending idle
time together, and the small remarks below, and it falls from fights, insults and slights. A
program avoids a rival's neighbourhood, and friends in the same dormitory or quarters are glad of
each other. When a friend is lost, let go, sold, fused or spent on a build, the programs that held
it dear carry a grudge about it. When a rival or enemy leaves, they feel better.

SOCIAL also shows how sociable the program is, Reserved, Sociable or Chatty, and what it is known
for among the others, such as good company or a brawler.

Chatter

Every so often the base pairs up idle staff standing close together, and each pair may speak. How
likely depends on the speaker's sociability, and what it says depends on how it feels about the
listener and on its own temperament. It might make small talk, talk shop, joke, compliment or thank
the other, commiserate, complain, slight or insult. Each remark leaves a memory on the listener,
and sometimes on the speaker. Gossip repeats a fondness or grudge held about a third program, and
is never passed on a second time. A sulking program is likelier to say the sour ones.

The TALK tab is the readable log of it: what the program has said and heard, newest first.

# Contracts and the Broker

A Contract Broker is a structure you build in [your base](your-base). Once one is standing, the
base menu grows a Contracts row, and that one screen does three separate things: it lists the jobs
you are holding, it lists the three the board is offering, and it is where a delivery is actually
handed over.

Reading a board and signing it are different questions. You can read the board from anywhere — four
frames down a Stack, halfway across the sector — but signing for a job means standing on your own
base floor. The screen's header says which of the two you are doing.

Every job on the screen carries three lines. The first is what it asks for, the second is the
flavour, and the third — the one with the arrow — is how and where it is satisfied. That third line
is the one to read when a job looks like it is doing nothing.

## Handing cargo over

This is the part that catches people out. A job that asks you to deliver something is **not**
satisfied by making the items, and it is **not** satisfied by putting them on a shelf. You have to
be carrying them, be standing at the counter the job was signed at, and pick that job's own row on
the contracts screen. The arrow line tells you which of those three you are still missing, and
counts what is in your pack once you are standing in the right place.

When you are in the right place with the right cargo, the base tab on the info column says so
without you having to open anything.

Everything else settles itself wherever you happen to be. Kills, descents, breaches, builds and the
rest are counted as they happen; only a delivery needs you at a counter.

## Whose job it is

Settlements out on the open ground post their own boards, and a job is delivered where it was
signed. A town's job is handed over at that town, and your Broker's is handed over at your Broker —
standing at the wrong one is why a row you are certain you have filled refuses to close.

A town posts work it cares about, and it will not deal with you at all if you have made an enemy of
it. Finishing its jobs is one of the things that moves its opinion the other way.

## Choosing what to take

You can hold three at once, and you can give one back with A if you have taken something you cannot
finish. Onboarding missions are the exception — they arrive on their own, they cannot be handed
back, and the next only arrives when the last one closes.

The board rotates on its own over time, and what it offers depends on how deep the sector is, so a
job you passed on is not necessarily gone forever. What you have already finished will not be
offered again unless it is one of the repeatable ones.

Contracts are also the one place progress is not earned by fighting: a job may pay experience for
[building, trading or descending](getting-stronger) just as readily as for a kill.

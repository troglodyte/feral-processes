# Before you breach

There is no way back through a portal, so the question of whether you are ready for the next zone is
worth asking before you build one rather than after you step through. What changes on the far side
is not just that everything hits harder. It is the shape of the fights.

Before zone 2, the thing to have is a routine that hits a whole group.

Zone 1 keeps a group to two programs at most, which is why a run's opening reads as a duel. Zone 2
lifts that ceiling to ten in a group and two groups on the field at once, though one fight never
fields more than eight programs in all. A party that only knows how to hit one target answers a
crowd one program at a time while the whole crowd answers back, and that arithmetic does not
improve by playing it better. A routine whose name ends in Group
is the answer, and it is worth going into the Stack for the Portal Fragments knowing you also need
the levels, the disks or the research to bring one across.

Through zone 10 the portal also needs that zone's Phase Key, dropped by Stack guardians, so a guardian kill comes before the build.

Take across as well:

- A party. Five can fight beside you, and past zone 1 you want the front slot filled by something
  that can hold it while the rest work.
- Power to spend. Group routines cost far more than a plain attack, and Power is what pays for them.
- Gear worth re-equipping. A piece locks in the level and zone you equipped it at, so re-equipping
  everything the moment you land is free strength.

Before zone 3, the thing to have is a routine that hits everything.

Zone 3 fields three groups and zone 4 fields four, which is the ceiling; a big party of your own reaches it sooner. Only the front two are ever
in reach of you at once — the rest can act only with routines that reach that far, and they come
forward as you clear the ones in front. A routine whose name ends in Everyone stops being a luxury
here, because a fight where three groups take turns being the front one is a fight decided by how
much of the field you can touch per turn.

Most of the routines that reach everything are on the research tree from the start, and they cost
Research Data and nothing else, so a base running through zone 2 can have one learned before you
arrive rather than something you set up after. One of them waits behind zone 3 itself.

A base is also something you defend from zone 2 on. Wild programs lay siege to it, so leave it
staffed and with defences before you step. See [sieges](sieges).

A breach takes nothing from you. Your pack, your currencies, your base and your research all cross
with you, so there is nothing to spend or sell first. One thing is worth checking before you
step:

- Check your companions against the ceiling. A companion has the same level cap you do, and it
  rises with the zone: 6 in zone 1, 12 in zone 2, then 11 more each zone. A program at the cap
  stops gaining levels until you cross, the same as you do.

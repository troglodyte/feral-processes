The base makes things.
Nothing makes you build it.

Every structure, every recipe, every rate in assets/ and crates/engine/, traced end to end — what feeds what, where the chain jams, and the specific numbers to move if a working base is meant to be the price of progress.

The finding

Breaching to the next sector needs a Home (build_cost: []) and a Zone Portal (10 Portal Fragments, from Stack guardians only). Neither is behind research; neither consumes anything the base produces. The entire eleven-machine production chain is optional, and Game::craft lets you hand-compile every item those machines make, at the same cost, with no worker and no adjacency.

Everything the base can make

Six independent chains. Only one of them — the bottom row — leads anywhere the game requires you to go, and it starts underground rather than at a machine.

Figure 2

The chain is starved, then it clogs

An extractor's yield is tier + (zone − 1) units per cycle at a 0.4 + 0.1 × tier success rate. An assembler's yield is hard-coded to one unit per cycle, forever — upgrade_structure never touches ticks_per_unit, and the completion branch writes += 1. So supply climbs 16× across a run while demand stays flat, and the ratio is only ever correct for about one sector.

Hierarchy

The one thing the base actually gates

Research Data has exactly one source in the game. Every structure below the first six, every field routine, and six gear recipes sit behind it — so the Research Node is the only genuine base dependency in the build. It is also the slowest thing in the game: Research Data is banked: true, which routes it past node_payout to a flat 1 per cycle, so upgrading a Research Node from Mk1 to Mk5 buys 1.8× and nothing more.

Analysis

Where it jams, and why nobody notices

Ordered by how much each one costs the stated goal — a base that must work for the run to continue. The first four are why the base is skippable; the rest are why it is unsatisfying when it isn't.

B1

The breach path never touches the base

A Home costs nothing (build_cost: []) and lands on the tile you founded from. A Zone Portal costs 10 Portal Fragments, ramped 50% per zone below the current one, and Portal Fragments drop from Stack guardians (4–8) and nowhere else. No research gates either structure. That is the whole of progression, and it consumes zero units of anything eleven assemblers and five extractors produce.

assets/structures/home.ron · assets/structures/portal.ron · lib.rs::zone_portal_cost · tuning::STACK_BOSS_PORTAL_FRAGMENT_DROP = 4..=8
B2

Hand-compiling makes every assembler optional

Game::craft resolves a bench with has_structure(kind) — does one exist anywhere, not are you standing beside it. It pays from the player's own inventory, needs no posted program, no adjacency, no buffer, no power, and costs one tick. The recipe it charges is the same craftable.cost the machine runs. Every single item the eleven assemblers make is obtainable this way at the identical price.

Four of those recipes — Power Cell, ICE Breaker, and the low-tier gear — carry no requires_structure at all, so they need no base whatsoever.

crates/engine/src/game/crafting.rs:127 has_structure · :232 craft · structures.rs::AssembleDef ("the machine runs that item's own ItemDef::craftable.cost")
B3

No first-stage assembler can be fed at Mk1

A Lathe eats 333 Core Fragments per 1,000 ticks. A Mk1 Mining Node in zone 1 produces 50. That is 6.7 nodes for one Lathe — and an assembler pulls from orthogonal neighbours only, so four is the physical ceiling and three is realistic once something downstream takes a side. Four Mk1 nodes hold a Lathe at 60% duty; the Compiler at 53%.

Each of those five machines also needs its own posted program, against an opening roster of three. So the first chain a player tries to build is short by roughly three feeders and two bodies, and the visible result is a machine that says Starved forever.

systems::node_payout · systems::mining_success_chance · base::collect::ORTHOGONAL · tuning::BASE_PET_CAPACITY = 3
B4

Extractors scale 16×; assemblers never scale at all

node_payout(tier, zone) = tier + (zone − 1) and success runs 0.5 → 0.9, so a Mining Node goes from 50 to 810 units per 1,000 ticks. The assembler completion branch is *stock.output.entry(product) += 1 and the comment above it confirms upgrade_structure never touches ticks_per_unit. Output is one unit per cycle in zone 1 and one unit per cycle in zone 5.

The Armory, Fabricator and Compiler each carry a five-tier upgrade path costing (12 CF + 1 CG) × N — 180 CF and 15 CG to reach Mk5 — and it buys five quality points per tier on hand-compiled gear and no throughput at all.

crates/engine/src/systems.rs:1270 (completion branch) · craft_quality_floor · tuning::QUALITY_BENCH_PER_TIER = 5
B5

The Mining Node is the worst source of Core Fragments

Three supplies compete. A Mk1 node posted with a program yields 0.05/tick. Cutting base rock yields 1 fragment at 25% per 12-tick swing — 0.021/tick, with no build cost, no power, no worker and no research. And seven of seventeen species carry work_resource: Some("core_fragment"), dropping 2–4 per kill: a single fight is worth 40–80 ticks of a Mk1 node.

Since fighting is the thing the design already wants, the node loses the comparison it was built to win.

tuning::BASE_MINE_FRAGMENT_CHANCE = 0.25 · BASE_DIG_TICKS_PER_SWING = 12 · WORK_RESOURCE_DROP = 2..=4 · 7 of 17 assets/species/*.ron
B6

Power is not a constraint, and the cap is on the wrong building

The Recharger Node costs 10 CF for +4 supply — 2.5 CF per unit, ungated, and max_deployed: 0 (unlimited). The Line Driver costs 30 CF + 6 Cache Grain for +6 — 5 CF and 1 CG per unit, behind 40 RD and zone 2, and capped at three. The cheap uncapped one strictly dominates the expensive capped one.

A base running one of every machine draws 33. Nine Rechargers is 90 CF, less than the build cost of three assemblers. The whole ledger is decoration.

assets/structures/recharger_node.ron · line_driver.ron · game/base/power.rs::ledger · StructureDef::max_deployed
B7

Roster slots are 10 Core Fragments for five

BASE_PET_CAPACITY's own doc argues that a cap of 3 is what makes posting a program cost something — "every program at a machine is one absent from the party". The Data Cache sells five slots for 10 CF with no max_deployed, so 30 CF buys eighteen slots and the tension the comment describes never materialises. What still binds is taming throughput, which is a combat cost rather than a base one.

tuning.rs::BASE_PET_CAPACITY = 3 (and its "Class base jobs" note) · assets/structures/data_cache.ron pet_slot_bonus: 5
B8

The player has infinite cargo, so the Depot is invisible

Inventory is a bare Vec<(ItemId, u32)> with no capacity field, and Game::grant_loot unconditionally returns the full quantity it was given — the "how much actually landed" return value is threaded through every caller and never once used to refuse anything. The Depot exists solely so a posted hauler can empty a clogged machine. Nothing about carrying, hauling, or storing is a decision the player ever makes.

components.rs:450 Inventory · game/turn.rs:1118 grant_loot · assets/structures/depot.ron
B9

The chain's terminal products have thin sinks

ICE Breaker has four supplies and the Compiler is the most expensive of them (3 CF by hand, 5 Credits at the Market, a 0.30 nest-cache drop). Routine Disks are one-shot. Hardened Shell and Trace Sniffer are mid-tier gear that drops from ordinary kills at 8–10%. Patch Routine is the only thing the base makes that helps in the field, and it costs 18 Core Fragments traced to raw for a 120-tick Mitigation buff.

The genuinely strong sinks — Recompile Kernel and the three permanent upgrade chips — all sit behind program_refactoring: 75 RD, zone 2, plus two machines. They arrive after the player has already decided whether the base matters.

assets/items/ice_breaker.ron (cache_drop: 0.3, no requires_structure) · black_market.ron · assets/research/program_refactoring.ron
B10

Contracts pay a 25% premium for processing

block_order pays 40 Credits for 8 Bytecode Blocks. Those blocks trace to 32 Core Fragments, which sell raw for 32 Credits. So a Refinery, a posted program and 96 machine-ticks are worth 8 Credits plus 100 XP. fragment_quota pays 35 Credits for 25 raw fragments worth 25 — a 10-Credit premium for hauling.

Contracts are the one place in the game where XP is granted for something other than fighting, and they are the cleanest sink the base has. They are priced as if they were a rounding error.

assets/contracts/block_order.ron, fragment_quota.ron, coil_order.ron, substrate_order.ron · Game::sell_price = item_value × sell_rate
B11

Three amenities author a power_draw nothing reads

base::power::ledger sums power_draw only where def.runs_a_job() — that is, only for structures declaring work or assembles. The Repair Bay (2), Log Analyzer Bay (2) and Sandbox (1) declare draw and run no job, so five units of authored cost are silently dropped. Either the field is meaningless on those three, or the ledger is missing them.

game/base/power.rs::ledger · StructureDef::runs_a_job · repair_bay.ron, defrag_bay.ron, sandbox.ron
B12

flat_payout is documented, supported, and used by nothing

The field exists on WorkDef, is documented at length in assets/structures/README.md, has a test named after it, and is authored by zero of the thirty shipped structure files. Its own doc comment describes the exact case the Compiler is: "a node producing something consumed one at a time — a taming catalyst, a key — instead outruns its own sink within a zone or two." ICE Breaker is the taming catalyst, and it is on the scaling curve.

structures.rs::WorkDef::flat_payout · rg flat_payout assets/ → 3 hits, all in READMEs
B13

access_shard is dead content

No craftable, no droppable, no cache_drop, no node produces it. Its only in-game source is the Saboteur's starting kit (2 units); its only sink is selling it for 12 Credits. Its own description says so: nothing in a frame asks for one any more.

assets/items/access_shard.ron · assets/classes/saboteur.ron — the only two files that name it outside docs

Recommendations

Ten levers, cheapest first

Each is scoped to the smallest change that moves the number. data means an edit inside assets/ and nothing else — no seam, no save bump, no schema change. code means engine work.

L1

Put base output in the Zone Portal's build cost

data

This is the single highest-leverage edit in the repo, and it is one line. portal.ron already sets zone_portal: true, which makes every entry in build_cost a per-zone rate ramped by ZONE_PORTAL_COST_GROWTH_PERCENT. Add chain products to it:

build_cost: [("portal_fragment", 10), ("cache_grain", 6), ("recompile_kernel", 2)],

Cache Grain has one source (the Cache Tap, 40 RD, zone 2) and it is the only material that survives a breach — so it is the natural currency of "you had a base last sector". Recompile Kernel forces the Annealing → Refactor Bench chain to have actually run. At zone 4 the ramp makes that 10 / 15 / 5, which is a real production target rather than a toll.

Answers B1 directly. After this, no breach happens without a working base.

Verify against tests/research.rs:820, which asserts nothing unlocks the portal — it does not constrain the cost.
L2

Cap the Recharger Node and the Data Cache

data

One field each. max_deployed: 2 on recharger_node.ron restores the Line Driver's reason to exist and makes the 33-unit whole-base draw a genuine constraint — 4 (Home) + 8 (two Rechargers) + 18 (three Line Drivers) = 30, which is just short, so the player has to choose which machines run.

max_deployed: 1 on data_cache.ron puts the roster at 8 and restores exactly the tension BASE_PET_CAPACITY's comment claims: every program at a machine is one absent from the party.

Answers B6 and B7. The field already exists and the Line Driver already uses it.

L3

Halve first-stage assembler inputs

data

An assembler runs its product's craftable.cost, so this is an edit to four item files, not four structure files. Take Blank Substrate, Bytecode Block and Annealed Core from 4 core_fragment to 2, and Logic Wafer from 4 raw_trace to 2. Drop each item's value to 2 to keep the recipe ceiling satisfied.

Feeder demand falls from 6.67 to 3.33 — buildable with three adjacent Mk1 nodes at 100% duty, which is exactly what fits on a grid. The Compiler's 3 CF → 2 CF does the same for ICE Breakers.

Answers B3. Alternatively raise MINING_SUCCESS_BASE from 0.40 to 0.55, which is one constant but moves every extractor and is code.

Re-run cargo test -p feral-processes-engine balance_sim and the value-ceiling census in tests/assets.rs.
L4

Raise contract rewards 2–3× on chain deliveries

data

block_order at 40 Credits should be 90–110; coil_order at 55 should be 130; substrate_order at 70 should be 150. These are the only sink in the game whose payout can be tuned freely — the recipe ceiling does not apply to a contract reward, because a contract is not a craft. It is also the only place XP is legally granted for something other than combat, which the seam notes already call a deliberate amendment.

Add repeatable chain-delivery contracts at zones 3–5 for Recompile Kernels and Trace Sniffers; there are none today above annealed_stock.

Answers B10 and half of B9. No code, no test to update beyond the assets census.

L5

Set flat_payout: true on the taming and key nodes

data

The field was built for this and is used nowhere. It is not the Compiler's problem today — the Compiler is an assembler and already flat — but if any node is ever added that produces ICE Breakers, Routine Disks or Portal Fragments directly, it needs this. Worth authoring now on at least one shipped structure so the field has a census entry and stops being invisible schema.

Answers B12.

L6

Price field rest behind the base

data

outlet.ron is 5 Core Fragments and carries no requires_structure, so the item that lets you rest anywhere costs less than a Mining Node and needs no base at all. Move it to cost: [("charge_coil", 1)] with requires_structure: Some("assembly_bay") and the Power chain suddenly has a reason to run every trip out.

This is the cleanest way to make the base a place you return to rather than a place you passed through, and it changes no code.

Answers the "why come back" half of B9.

L7

Scale assembler output by tier

code

One line in assembler_system's completion branch: += 1 becomes += tier, with StructureTier added to the query. That gives the Armory, Fabricator and Compiler upgrade paths a throughput meaning — today 180 CF and 15 CG buys twenty quality points — and stops the zone-5 clog by letting the consuming side scale with the producing side.

Pair it with upgrade blocks on the six assemblers that have none, so the whole chain has one lever instead of a scaling half and a flat half.

Answers B4. Watch: it also multiplies the value the chain destroys, so re-check the recipe ceiling.

crates/engine/src/systems.rs, the *stock.output.entry(product).or_default() += 1 line.
L8

Make requires_structure mean proximity, or add machine_only

code

Two shapes, and they close B2 from opposite ends.

  • Proximity. Change Game::craft's bench resolution from has_structure(kind) to an adjacency check. Small, but it collides with best_structure_tier, whose doc explicitly argues a bench is "a thing the base owns, not a thing the player walks to" — so this is a seam change and belongs in docs/seams.md first.
  • machine_only: bool on CraftableDef. #[serde(default)], set on the five intermediates, and the recipe simply stops appearing in the craft menu. Moddable, additive, no save bump, no seam moved. This is the better shape.

Answers B2, which is the reason the eleven assemblers currently read as convenience.

L9

Give the player a cargo cap

code

The plumbing is already there and unused: Game::grant_loot returns "how much landed", every caller handles a partial, and no caller ever gets one. Add a capacity to Inventory, have grant_loot return less than it was asked for when full, and the Depot becomes the answer to a question the player is actually asked.

This is the largest single change on the list and the one most likely to make a base feel load-bearing, because it converts the base from a factory into a home. It needs a save field and a HUD row, so it earns the spec-and-plan pipeline.

Answers B8.

L10

Decide what the three inert power_draw values mean

code

Either delete power_draw from repair_bay.ron, defrag_bay.ron and sandbox.ron — they are honest zero-draw amenities — or widen ledger to sum draw for any structure that declares it, which adds 5 to the whole-base total and makes the amenities compete with the machines. The second reads better with L2, since a capped grid makes "do I power the Sandbox or the Lathe" a real choice.

Answers B11. Whichever way, a census test should pin it.

If only three ship

L1, L2 and L8. L1 makes the base structurally required for progression, L2 makes its constraints real, and L8 makes the machines the only route to what it produces. Together they are two data edits and one #[serde(default)] field — under a day's work — and they change the answer to "do I need a base?" from no to yes, every sector.

Provenance

Where every number came from

Structures
All 30 files in assets/structures/, read whole. Schema and field semantics from crates/engine/src/structures.rs.
Items
All 64 files in assets/items/. Recipes are craftable.cost; prices are value resolved through Game::sell_price.
Research
All 25 files in assets/research/; costs and prerequisites parsed directly, totals summed.
Extractor yield
systems::node_payout (tier + NODE_PAYOUT_ZONE_BONUS × (zone−1)) × systems::mining_success_chance (MINING_SUCCESS_BASE 0.4 + 0.1 × tier), over WorkDef::ticks_per_unit. Species base_int, base_speed, morale, need strain and the Keen Scavenger perk all shift these and are held at baseline throughout.
Assembler yield
One unit per AssembleDef::ticks_per_unit, from the completion branch of systems::assembler_system. Input demand is the product's own recipe over the same cycle.
Adjacency

What this is blind to

  • Play. Nothing here was observed in a session. Every rate is derived from the constants, not measured. docs/measurements/ has no entry for base throughput.
  • Sorties. The Dispatch Relay returns loot from off-screen fights that this analysis does not price, and that could be a meaningful Core Fragment supply.
  • The Stack market. STACK_MARKET_* constants imply a second trade surface underground with its own prices, not traced here.
  • Work orders. settle_orders and schedule_base_labour decide which machines get bodies. Every duty-cycle figure above assumes a program is posted and stays posted; needs, OffShift and Downed all pull bodies off jobs and would lower every number.
  • Player speed. All cycle lengths use DEFAULT_BASE_SPEED = 10; the shipped roster spans 6 to 14, which moves a cycle ±20% (WORK_TICKS_PER_SPEED = 0.05).

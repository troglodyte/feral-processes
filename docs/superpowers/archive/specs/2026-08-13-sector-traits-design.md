# Sector Traits

**Status:** implemented. Audited 2026-09-02 against the source tree, not against this header. See `../../INDEX.md`.

## The problem

A breach currently changes numbers and nothing else.

`ZoneLevel` gates enemy stats and group cap, gear scaling, node payout,
portal build cost, boss loot bands, and `upgrade_ceiling = min(max_tier,
zone)`. That last one is the only thing resembling an unlock, and Mk*N* is a
bigger version of a machine you already own.

What a breach does *not* change is the sector itself. `WorldMap::new(seed)`
takes a seed and nothing else, so a zone-7 sector is generated by exactly the
same rules as zone 1: the same six biomes at the same five thresholds. The
species roster is filtered by habitat only. Every structure, item, recipe and
research node is available from turn one, and Research Data survives a
breach — so the tech tree could in principle be finished without ever
breaching.

Against that, a breach *costs* build salvage, breach keys and every trader's
shelf. The deal on offer is: lose your stockpile, keep your base, fight
harder things, get Mk+1.

This spec covers the first half of the answer — making a sector *a different
place*. The second half, a guarded site that pays a permanent unlock, is a
separate spec (see **Not in scope**).

## What a sector trait is

`assets/sectors/*.ron` becomes a real content directory alongside species,
structures, items, abilities and affixes. One file is one `SectorDef`:

- `id`, `name`, `description` — the last two player-facing.
- `shape` — a delta per `classify` threshold.
- `palette` — two hues, ground and hazard.

Nothing else. The engine names no sector. A mod adds one by dropping in a
file, and **deleting the directory restores today's game exactly**, the way
deleting `assets/affixes/` or `assets/policies/enemy_battle.ron` does. A
malformed file is skipped with a logged warning, never a panic, following
`SpeciesDb::load_dir`.

### Which trait a zone gets

Derived, never stored: a function of `(world seed, zone)`, both of which are
already saved. So it survives a reload with **no `SAVE_FORMAT_VERSION` bump
and no cache**, and a different world reads differently for free. This is
`descriptions.rs`'s argument applied to terrain, and it carries the same
three traps:

- **Never `GameRng`.** A draw does not survive a save/load and shifts every
  later roll in the run. This is the same rule world generation already
  follows for Stack frames, entrance placement and lair species.
- **Never a bare `StdRng` sequence.** Its stream is not stable across a
  `rand` upgrade, so a dependency bump would silently reshuffle every sector
  in the game. A local `StdRng` seeded per-sector is fine for *drawing*;
  what must not be relied on is the sequence position.
- **Never a caller-supplied seed.** Two call sites then drift on how they
  salt.

If you ever find yourself adding a save field or a cache for a sector's
trait, something has started reading run state it shouldn't.

### Zone 1 is always neutral

Zone 1 uses today's thresholds and today's palette verbatim, bypassing the
roll entirely.

This is not politeness. `in_opening_ring` fields only species that
`beatable_by_a_fresh_player` clears, and `habitat_pools` falls back to the
biome's *unfiltered* roster when nothing qualifies — so a trait that biased
zone 1's biome mix would move the opening roster while looking like a
cosmetic change, and could empty the ring while leaving it looking intact.
`the_shipped_roster_has_species_on_both_sides_of_the_opening_ring` is the
existing census that a biased zone 1 could break from either end.

## The world-shape half

`WorldMap::classify` is five hardcoded thresholds over three Perlin fields:

```
e < -0.3            -> DataVoid
e >  0.55           -> BlackIce
t < -0.3            -> StaticField
t >  0.3 && m < -0.1 -> NullSector
m >  0.15           -> Mainframe
otherwise           -> OpenGrid
```

A trait carries a delta per threshold. `WorldMap` gains a `SectorShape` field
set at construction; `classify` reads it instead of the constants, and
today's numbers become the neutral shape.

That is the entire mechanism, and it is deliberately the *only* knob. A
sector where `t < -0.3` becomes `t < 0.1` is mostly Static Field, which means
it looks different, fights different and builds different — from one number:

- **Look** falls out because most of the screen is one biome.
- **Roster** falls out because `habitat_pools` filters species by the tile's
  biome. There is deliberately **no separate species-pool bias**; a second
  knob pointing at the same outcome could disagree with the first.
- **Building** falls out because walkable ground moved, and
  `Biome::walkable` is the one predicate that decides where anything is
  placed.

### A sector must leave ground to stand on

Nothing about a threshold delta stops an authored sector generating a map
that is almost entirely `DataVoid` and `BlackIce`. That is not merely ugly:
`enter_next_zone` calls `find_walkable_start` on the new map, every spawn,
structure and Stack link refuses an unwalkable tile, and `stamp_platform`
needs somewhere to lay the base. A sector with no ground is a stranded run.

So load-time validation samples the shape over a fixed region and refuses a
sector whose walkable fraction falls below a floor, logging a warning and
skipping the file like any other malformed `.ron`. The floor belongs in
`tuning.rs` — it is a playability bound, not content — alongside the other
knobs the engine hardcodes.

This is the same class of census as
`every_biome_a_stack_link_can_open_in_fields_a_boss`: a data-driven
constraint that reads as a tuning edit and strands the player if it is wrong.

### Known cost

An existing save's *unexplored* chunks regenerate under the new thresholds,
so a mid-run map can change shape at a chunk boundary the player has not
walked to. `WorldMap` caches chunks it has generated and saves its
`overrides` overlay, so walked ground and anything stamped is unaffected —
but the join will be visible to a player who breached before the update and
explores after it.

Accepted rather than hidden. The alternative is gating traits to zones
entered after the update, which requires a saved field and buys a
one-release cosmetic discontinuity.

## The palette half

The renderer's colour table carries an explicit rule, stated in
`biome_tint`'s doc comment in `crates/gui/src/render/base.rs`:

> **hue answers "can I cross this", pattern answers "what is it".**

The walkable biomes stay in a cool family; the two that are holes in the map
go hot amber. The generated grounds are spread across **brightness rather
than hue**, because hue is already spoken for by that rule. Platform is
deliberately much the darkest, having been taken down twice after being seen
on screen behind a full base.

So a per-sector palette may not move biomes around freely. It moves the two
*bands* and leaves everything inside them alone:

**Work in HSV over the existing table as the reference. Keep each biome's
saturation and value exactly; replace its hue** with the sector's ground hue
(walkable) or hazard hue (unwalkable).

That is what makes it safe. The brightness spread that separates Platform /
NullSector / OpenGrid / Mainframe / StaticField is what the current table
uses to tell them apart, and it survives untouched. The cool/warm split stops
being seven authored colours and becomes two authored numbers, which is a far
smaller surface for an authoring mistake.

Load-time validation refuses a ground hue outside the cool band or a hazard
hue outside the warm band — skipped with a logged warning, like every other
malformed `.ron`.

**Textures are not touched.** `draw_traces`, `draw_dot`, `draw_broken_grid`,
`draw_speckle`, `draw_slab`, `draw_depth` and `draw_shards` are each biome's
identity, closer to a glyph than to decoration. Varying them per sector means
relearning the map on every breach, which is the opposite of the goal.

## Plumbing

The renderer never touches the ECS `World`, and this must not become the
reason it starts. The palette arrives through the map view app-core already
builds: `biome_tint` stops being a free function over a hardcoded match and
becomes a lookup against a palette the view carries. One new field on the
view struct, one signature change in `render/base.rs`. No new dependency and
no new seam.

`biome_tint` must **stay exhaustive** for the same reason it is today: a new
`Biome` should not compile until someone has decided which side of the
walkability rule it falls on. Moving the source of the colours does not
relax that.

The sector's name and description surface where the zone level is already
announced, so a breach says where you have landed rather than only how hard
it is.

## Testing

- **The generalised tint rule.** `every_biomes_tint_says_whether_it_can_be_
  walked_on` today asserts the table is right. It becomes a sweep over every
  shipped sector asserting that **every** sector's table is right. This is
  the gate that stops a mod painting BlackIce cool green, and it is the
  reason the transform is a constrained band swap rather than a free palette.
- **Determinism.** The same `(seed, zone)` derives the same trait across a
  save/load. Assert against a real round trip, not a recomputation in the
  same process.
- **Zone 1 neutrality.** A census asserting zone 1's shape and palette equal
  the neutral values, so the opening ring is provably unmoved.
- **Empty directory.** With `assets/sectors/` removed, generation and colours
  are byte-identical to the neutral shape — the same absence-is-supported
  property affixes and the enemy policy have. An omission is invisible
  otherwise.
- **Load robustness.** A malformed sector file, an out-of-band hue and a
  shape that leaves too little walkable ground are all skipped with a
  warning rather than panicking.
- **Every shipped sector leaves ground to stand on.** A census over the real
  assets, asserting the walkable floor holds for each — the gate that stops
  a retune stranding a run.
- **`cargo test -p feral-processes-engine balance_sim`.** The roster mix
  shifts with the biome mix, and that gate exists for exactly this.
- **`cargo test --workspace`** is the final gate.

## Not in scope

**Salvage Sites**, the second half of the answer, get their own spec: one
boss-guarded site per zone whose clearance unlocks a *found-only* research
node — `ResearchDef` gaining `found: bool` (`#[serde(default)]`, so no
version bump and no mod breakage), a node that never appears in the `T` menu
and cannot be bought at any price. That reuses the entire existing unlock
pipeline (`unlocks_structures` / `unlocks_recipes` / `unlocks_abilities`)
rather than inventing one, and it is what makes content genuinely exist in
zone N that did not exist in zone 1.

Two decisions already settled for that spec, recorded here so they are not
relitigated:

- **The site must be fought for.** "Progression is earned by fighting" is the
  design spine — scan, the Terminal, free rest and the Market's fragment
  listing were all closed to establish it. A landmark you walk onto and
  collect violates it; a boss-guarded one is consistent with the Stack lair
  that already exists.
- **The site pays no Portal Fragments.** Breach pacing stays untouched, and
  the site is a reason to stay and work a sector rather than a shortcut out
  of it. The known Portal Fragment softlock (a zone fields two lairs; a
  `black_ice_pick` costs 18) is left for a fix of its own rather than
  absorbed into this.

Also out of scope, and deliberately: choosing where a breach lands (the
ladder stays linear), per-sector texture families, and any zone-local
advantage that does not travel.

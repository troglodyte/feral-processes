Feral Processes

Tame the rogue programs you fight. Compile them into a base that runs itself. Then descend the Stack.

A cyberpunk simulation of the Grid. Collect the monsters you beat and raise them. Build a base and staff it with programs who remember what you did to them. Go down into first-person dungeons for the loot nothing on the surface has. The sim is headless Rust, kept separate from the renderer that draws it.

What you actually do

Fight it, compile it, put it to work

Anything you beat can join your roster, and everything on your roster is labour you don't have to do yourself.

01 — COMBAT

Intrusions

Your party fights theirs in rounds, against grouped enemies, and only the front groups can reach you. Wear a hostile down and Decompile it to add it to your roster. Up to five can fight beside you. Press R to fast-resolve a fight you already know you've won.

02 — BATTLE MAPS

Or fight it on a grid

Turn on tactical battles and a fight in the open is played on a board instead. It has cover, line of sight, opportunity attacks, ranged and wide-swing weapons, and full friendly fire. Five wild programs of one kind merge into a single squad that takes up four tiles.

03 — YOUR CHARACTER

A character sheet you spend

Pick one of eight classes. Each level gives you six points to put into five attributes: Parity, Analysis, Footprint, Bandwidth and Persistence. Before you commit, the Points screen shows how the spend changes hit chance, damage per swing and swings-to-win. Perks and talent trees sit on top of that.

04 — ROUTINES

Routines, not spells

Routines install into slots that open as you level. There are over a hundred. You can fork copies of yourself, raise the fallen as temporary allies, teleport, or fight as a species whose image you've learned. Some, like Prompt Injection and Hallucination, attack an enemy's AI directly.

05 — PRODUCTION

Lines, not single machines

Machines feed each other by touching. Each connected run of machines is one production line with one worker, who pulls from the end nearest the finished product. Leave a gap to split a line in two and each half gets its own worker.

06 — RESEARCH

Study what you catch

Research is a project, and Research Nodes work on it. Past the first sector, a node needs a live subject pinned in the Research Station's pen, and finishing the node uses the subject up. Most of the tree stays hidden until a study turns it up.

07 — EXTRACTION

Take a program apart

You carry a beaten program home whole, with its level, rarity and condition. A Quarantine Rack holds it until a Teardown Rig breaks it down. Strip it for components, tap its core, pull its gear, or extract its image so you can fight as it later.

08 — AWAY TEAMS

Sorties and honeypots

Staff you aren't fielding can go on sorties from a Dispatch Relay. They fight real battles off-screen and come home with loot, or come home with fewer of them. Set a honeypot and it catches a weaker wild program while you're out.

09 — STAKES

Permadeath, or Forgiving

You pick one per run. On Forgiving, a fallen program is benched, not destroyed. On Permadeath, a run that flatlines seals its own save. Achievements are the only thing that carries over between runs, and they pay out at the start of the next one.

Staff management

Your crew remembers, and holds grudges

Every program you own has a personality, a memory and opinions about the others. They notice when a machine is always backed up, when a post is out of reach, when they get mauled, and when they win against the odds. Fighting side by side builds friendships, and watching a crewmate turn on one of you builds feuds.

  • A SOCIAL tab for every program. Opinions run from Enemy to Close, and you can read the conversations in the TALK tab.
  • Gossip and feuds. Idle programs talk to each other, and how sociable a program is shapes who it ends up liking.
  • Moods with reasons. Standing next to a rival, an unpowered post, or a long shift all show up as thoughts you can read.
  • Sulkers act out. A sulking program refuses the job it resents, sabotages its output and won't stand beside a rival. Push it far enough and it stops working altogether.
  • Needs. Base staff use up Coherence and Slack as they work, and a program that runs low walks itself to a Sandbox or a Log Analyzer Bay without being told.

Basebuilding

A base that works while you're away

Mark sites, dig out the rock, lay out floors and walls, and set up production lines. Work orders queue up the materials and can be reordered, and Depots hold the stock that lines can't make themselves. It all keeps running whether or not you're standing there.

SIEGES

They come to you

From the second sector on, wild programs walk into your base. If you're home, the fight plays out on a battle map built from your own layout, and your machines are obstacles that can be smashed. If you're away, it resolves without you, so build Turrets and Walls.

GEAR

A Mod Bench

Add affixes to your gear and strip them off again. The bench has its own research tree, and Deflection is a stat only gear can give you.

THREATS

The GC Entropy Sweep

Every so often the Grid's garbage collector sweeps through. It's rare and you get a warning first, which leaves a few minutes to get ready. The first sector never sees one.

Settlements

The Grid has neighbours, and they keep score

The world seed places the towns, and you find one by walking into it. A town is a 3×3 block and a city can be up to 7×7. Each has a counter that buys and sells, a temperament that sets prices both ways, and a specialty it leads with. What it does for you after that depends on what you've done for it.

Standing moves on deeds, not points. Finish a job for a town, clear a nest near it or collapse a Stack on its doorstep, and it warms to you. Take a contract and abandon it, and it cools. You can reach either end of the ladder in one run.

Hostile Closes its market and raids your base for a share of your banked salvage. It also sends patrols near you and takes a cut from any caravan route that passes through.
Cold Still trades, but thinly, and posts a single job.
Neutral Where every town starts: a counter and two jobs on the board.
Warm Three jobs and better prices. Its specialty leads what it posts.
Allied Four jobs and a detachment stationed near your base. It will give you one of its own programs if you ask, and lend you a link home and back out again.

Towns change over time. Every Server is due to grow into a Mainframe city on a date the seed has already set. Trading brings that date forward, but only so far, so money alone can't found one. A neglected city starves back down without reverting to what it was.

OUTPOSTS

Found your own

Research Outpost Founding and you can plant an Outpost Kit out on the surface. An outpost grows as you staff it, can be raided, and can be the end of a caravan route.

CARAVAN ROUTES

Cargo that sells itself

Load a manifest at the Dispatch Relay and name a town or outpost you know, and Credits come home. A route keeps running until you cut it, even across hostile ground.

Old-school dungeon crawling

The place notices what you take from it

Every zone has links down into the Stack. Its frames are procedurally generated and drawn in first person, one step and one turn at a time, and you map them as you go. The fights are harder down there and the rewards are better.

  • Doors, sealed vaults and caches, with breakpoints, faults and corrupted ground in between.
  • Orphaned programs waiting to be adopted.
  • A seeded guardian in the deepest room. Beat it and the stack collapses, and the way back out goes with it.
  • A Trace meter that rises with everything you take, and it's what hunts you.

The world is one continuous map for the whole run. A breach raises the danger tier rather than rebuilding the sector. The only way to breach is a portal you pay for, using fragments that only the Stack drops.

Install

Clone it and run it

You need the Rust toolchain. The debug build is playable here, and cargo run is the intended way to start the game.

The binary finds assets/, saves/ and your profile relative to the checkout it was built from. Leave the clone where it is, even if you cargo install --path crates/launcher to put it on your PATH.

Moddable by design

Content is a file, not a code change

Species, structures, items, routines, contracts, towns, outposts, sortie sites, classes, talents, research, memories, thoughts, conversations, needs, attributes, floors and the rock under your base are all plain .ron data. A malformed file is skipped with a warning rather than crashing the game. New fields are always optional, so existing mods keep parsing.

assets/species/my_program.ron
(
    id: "null_pointer",
    name: "Null Pointer",
    glyph: 'n',
    color: Magenta,
    base_hp: 96,
    base_atk: 18,
    habitats: [Backplane, Deadlock],
    growth_multiplier: 1.4,
    moves: [
        (name: "Pointer Stab", power: 8, spread: 3),
    ],
    abilities: [(id: "segfault", level: 6)],
)

Drop a 16×16 PNG named after a species into assets/sprites/ and the map draws it in place of the glyph. The sprite is tinted, so it picks up the danger colouring, the biome tint and the damage dimming automatically. Name it <name>.colour.png instead and it keeps its own colours. You can also draw sprites in the game, in the Sprite Forge.

Difficulty tuning is deliberately not data. Every number the engine hardcodes, from scaling curves and XP to drop rates, siege pressure and need decay, is a documented constant in crates/engine/src/tuning.rs. You can mod the content, but not how hard the game is.
